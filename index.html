Nutrition and Healthy Food
Awareness System

A Smart Awareness Platform for Better Food Choices

Students often choose meals based on taste, convenience, cost, or availability without knowing whether the food provides a balanced amount of nutrients. In college canteens, hostels and messes, students may also have limited awareness about nutrients, food labels, portion balance and healthier alternatives. The Nutrition and Healthy Food Awareness System aims to make nutrition information simple, accessible and useful for everyday food decisions.

Official Problem Statement

What Students Face Daily

Students face difficulty understanding whether their regular food choices are nutritionally balanced. Limited nutrition awareness, confusing food labels, lack of simple guidance and dependence on easily available canteen or mess food can lead to poor food choices. There is a need for a simple system that helps students understand nutrition and make more informed food choices.

Key Observation: Canteen menus and hostel messes default to taste, convenience, and refined carbs over nutrient density.

Nutrition Facts

Food Label Decoded Guide

Click any item on the Nutrition Facts label on the left to see practical student advice.

  • 5% or Less: Low in that nutrient (Good for Sodium, Saturated Fat, Added Sugars).
  • 20% or More: High in that nutrient (Look for this in Dietary Fiber, Protein, Vitamins).
  • WHO Limit: Keep daily added sugar under 25g and sodium under 2,000mg.
Feature 5

Portion Balance Tips • The Student Plate Model

How to balance your mess thali or canteen plate without weighing food

🥗 50% of Plate

Vegetables & Salads

Fill half your plate with raw cucumbers, carrots, tomatoes, and cooked greens. Provides dietary fiber, micronutrients, and prevents lethargy.

🧀 25% of Plate

Quality Protein

Dal, paneer, sprouts, boiled eggs, or roasted chana. Essential for muscle repair, sustained attention in lectures, and satiety.

🌾 25% of Plate

Complex Carbohydrates

Whole wheat rotis, brown rice, millets (jowar/bajra), or oats. Provides steady, slow-releasing glucose for your brain without sugar crashes.
